**Session:** AI and Big Data Innovations in Transportation System Management<br>
**Panelists:** Darcy Bullock, Lyndsay Quist, and Seval Oz<br>
**Moderator:** Eric Clark<br>
**Event:** Purdue + Google AI Summit 2026<br>
**Date:** October 29, 2026

The available recording captures part of a transportation panel discussing how AI and large datasets can support engineering decisions. It begins and ends mid-sentence, so these notes cover the recorded excerpt rather than the full session.

## Validate measurements before modelling

The discussion emphasized that AI does not replace basic engineering and physical reasoning. Large volumes of measurements still need checks against the properties of the hardware, software, and physical system that produced them.

This matters when combining different data sources. Before interpreting a pattern, engineers need to understand what each measurement represents and how the collection methods might affect the result. More records alone do not establish that a model’s conclusion is reliable.

## Feed vehicle data back into road design

Darcy Bullock discussed how vehicle measurements could help examine existing design practices, particularly in construction zones. The examples connected road roughness, lateral motion, acceleration, and possible indicators of crash risk.

The role described for AI was to help identify patterns and direct engineering attention toward locations or design choices worth investigating. Those patterns can then inform further investigation and feed back into the design process.

The panel also stressed learning across states and institutions. Sharing data and experience can help agencies compare approaches rather than treating every local deployment as an isolated experiment.

## Be explicit about the safety objective

Another part of the discussion contrasted reducing the consequences of crashes with avoiding collisions. These objectives are related, but they call for different measurements and evaluation criteria.

For autonomous systems, the question is therefore broader than whether an algorithm performs well on one metric. Evaluation needs to connect the chosen metric to the intended safety outcome and to the conditions in which the system will operate.

## Move toward demonstrations that test deployment conditions

The excerpt also discussed moving beyond limited pilots toward demonstrations that reveal whether a technology can operate at a useful scale. High-speed autonomous racing appeared as an example of a demanding environment for studying vehicle dynamics and rapid decisions.

The broader message was that engineering validation, institutional coordination, and deployment evidence need to develop alongside the AI methods themselves.
